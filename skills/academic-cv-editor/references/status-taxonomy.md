# Academic Status Taxonomy

Use these labels conservatively and literally.

## Research interest
A topic the applicant is interested in. It is not evidence of a completed research project.

## Course paper
A paper completed primarily as coursework. It may still be strong and worth listing, but do not relabel it as independent research without evidence.

## Research paper
A completed research paper. This does not imply publication, submission, or external review.

## Selected research paper
A research paper deliberately highlighted in the CV. This is a presentation label, not a publication status.

## Working paper
A substantive manuscript still being developed. Use only when the project has advanced beyond a vague idea or short assignment.

## Conference paper
A paper prepared for conference submission or presentation. Clarify whether it is merely prepared, submitted, accepted, or presented.

## Submitted
The manuscript/abstract/paper has been formally submitted. No acceptance is implied.

## Accepted
The relevant venue has formally accepted it. Do not imply presentation has already occurred.

## Presented
The work has actually been delivered at the conference/event.

## Forthcoming
Use for a publication that has been formally accepted for publication and is awaiting publication. Do not use for submitted manuscripts.

## Published
The work is formally published and should be citable as such.

## In press
Use only when the publisher/venue itself supports that status.

## Under review
Use only if the work is actually under editorial/peer review and the applicant wants to disclose it. Do not infer this from mere submission.

## Independent status dimensions

Do not model academic outputs as a single promotion ladder. Record separately:
- Origin: coursework, thesis, independent work, or collaborative project.
- Manuscript maturity: proposed, in progress, complete, or explicitly designated working paper.
- Journal status: not submitted, submitted, under review (confirmed), accepted/forthcoming, published.
- Conference status: abstract submitted/accepted; paper submitted/accepted; scheduled presentation; actually presented.
- Contribution: authorship and actual responsibilities, including coauthors where supplied.

An accepted abstract does not establish full-paper peer review. A presented paper may remain unpublished. A course paper may later be published without losing its coursework origin. Preserve the relevant combination; never infer one dimension from another. Journal acceptance may be listed under Publications as accepted/forthcoming, clearly distinguished from published work. Planned talks belong under Upcoming Presentations, not completed presentations. Never invent bibliographic metadata to complete a citation.
