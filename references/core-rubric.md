# Core Academic CV Rubric

## 1. Research identity first

The first third of the CV should make the applicant's research direction legible when the record supports it. A strong research identity can be represented as:

`field -> subfield -> substantive problem -> mechanisms/themes -> empirical focus -> methods`

Do not force unrelated projects into a false single narrative. If the applicant has several genuine strands, label one as primary and the others as secondary/exploratory when useful.

## 2. Academic substance over résumé language

Do not default to corporate résumé conventions such as one-page compression, compulsory metrics, generic impact statements, or aggressive action verbs.

Prefer information that lets an academic reader evaluate the work:
- research question or object;
- cases and temporal scope;
- concepts or theory;
- evidence and sources;
- research design and methods;
- actual intellectual or technical contribution;
- outputs and recognition.

Good pattern:

`Analysed [evidence] using [method/approach] to examine [research problem].`

Weak pattern:

`Conducted comprehensive research and gained valuable analytical skills.`

## 3. Precision before prestige

The skill is an editor, not a hype generator. Prefer narrow, verifiable statements over prestigious but vague claims.

Avoid unsupported terms such as:
- groundbreaking;
- pioneering;
- novel;
- expert;
- advanced expertise;
- spearheaded;
- transformed;
- revolutionised;
- highly influential;
- internationally recognised.

## 4. Contribution calibration

Keep verbs proportional to actual contribution.

| Evidence supplied | Safe wording | Do not silently upgrade to |
|---|---|---|
| reviewed literature | reviewed / synthesised literature | developed the theoretical framework |
| helped collect data | assisted with / contributed to data collection | conducted the empirical analysis |
| contributed to design | contributed to research design | designed the study |
| team member | contributed to / worked on | led / directed |
| presented poster | presented / contributed to poster | published findings |

## 5. Undergraduate / early-career calibration

A strong early-career academic CV should demonstrate:
- intellectual seriousness;
- increasing independence;
- methodological awareness;
- research trajectory;
- initiative;
- credible outputs;
- readiness for more advanced research.

It should not imply mature scholarly authority that the record does not support.

## 6. Fact consistency audit

Before substantive rewriting, compare every supplied source for conflicts in:
- names and spellings;
- institutions and locations;
- degree titles and dates;
- GPA / ranking / test scores;
- role dates and titles;
- supervisors;
- project titles;
- award names and years;
- publication and conference status;
- methods and language levels.

If sources conflict, surface the conflict. Do not resolve it by guesswork.

## 7. Section rules

### Header
Normally include name, email, academic/personal website if relevant, location, and optionally phone. Do not add photo, age, sex, marital status, or full street address unless the user or local convention requires it.

### Research Profile
Use only when it materially clarifies identity. Usually 2-4 lines. It should answer "Who is this researcher?" rather than state a generic objective.

### Education
Include academically useful information: degrees, institutions, dates, GPA/marks if helpful, exchange study, relevant academic programmes, and standardized tests when appropriate. Secondary school is normally omitted once university-level evidence is stronger.

### Research Interests
Do not use as an unlimited keyword list. Remove duplicates and keep a coherent hierarchy of subfield, problem, mechanisms/themes, and regional/empirical focus.

### Research Experience
Each entry should communicate as many of these as the record supports:
1. research object;
2. applicant activity;
3. methods/evidence;
4. intellectual contribution.

### Selected Research Papers & Working Papers
Use for papers that demonstrate research capacity. A strong entry may include:
- title / citation;
- status;
- 2-4 domain keywords;
- 1-2 lines of substantive description;
- conference/award status.

Do not list every course essay.

### Publications
Keep formal publications distinct from papers, manuscripts, conference submissions, and presentations.

### Current Research Agenda
Activate only when the applicant can state a genuine research puzzle, substantive scope/cases, conceptual or theoretical logic, and a plausible research design/evidence strategy. Do not manufacture one from a broad interest.

### Awards & Honours
Prioritize research awards, academic scholarships, research fellowships, and academically selective honours. Explain the associated paper/project when that adds meaning.

### Academic Leadership
Keep leadership here when it has academic substance: research design, academic materials, conference programme design, training, assessment, curriculum-like work, or scholarly coordination. Move generic logistics/social activity elsewhere or compress it.

### Professional Experience / University Service
Retain when it demonstrates relevant substantive exposure, data/evidence work, institutional knowledge, responsibility, or transferable research skills. Keep low-relevance detail short.

### Methods
Use concrete method names. Distinguish `used`, `developing`, and `familiar with` when needed. Do not inflate exposure into proficiency.

## 8. Space allocation

Use the internal principle:

`section value ≈ relevance × evidence of ability × distinctiveness`

This is not a numerical score; it is a prioritization heuristic.

Academic relevance, not prestige, determines space.

## 9. Page length

Do not enforce a one-page rule. One or two pages can both be appropriate for undergraduate and early-career academic CVs. Cut low-value content before shrinking fonts or margins.

## 10. Conservative editing

Preserve:
- meaning;
- factual nuance;
- methodological information;
- uncertainty;
- status distinctions.

Change only when accuracy, relevance, readability, or structure improves.

## 11. Language

Prefer precise, compact, discipline-appropriate prose. Accuracy is more important than lexical variation. Repeating `analysed` is better than replacing it with an inaccurate stronger verb.

## 12. Final checks

Before finalizing, verify:
- factual integrity;
- academic credibility;
- research identity;
- research substance;
- method calibration;
- status labels;
- target relevance;
- redundancy;
- language consistency;
- visual integrity if rendered.
